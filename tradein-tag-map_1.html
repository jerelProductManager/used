The quote configurator emits nothing but a page_view.

Moving the condition slider swung the offer on this lens from $240 to $205 — a 15% price move, the single largest lever in the whole trade-in experience — and fired zero events. Not gtag, not Universal Analytics, nothing. The same is true of the accessory checkboxes, Select all, and Add Accessories. You can see that a seller reached step 2 and, later, whether a quote was created; you cannot see what condition they chose, whether they moved the slider at all, or where inside the step they gave up.

Findings

T1 · critical

Step 2 of the quote flow is a black box

The condition selector and accessory picker — everything that determines the offer a seller is shown — produce no analytics of any kind. I confirmed this by wrapping both gtag and ga and driving the slider through three positions: the displayed offer changed each time, the capture array stayed empty.

Concretely, this blocks: condition mix by category (are sellers self-reporting condition 5 and getting corrected on inspection?), whether slider engagement predicts quote completion, and any read on where inside step 2 abandonment happens. Those are the questions that decide whether the condition UI is doing its job, and none of them are answerable from GA4 today.

T2 · critical

Seven tracked components across the entire trade-in flow

The complete inventory, from the shipped JavaScript plus the data-tracking attributes rendered into the markup:

  • Category Groups — label carries each of the nine sell-categories
  • search / search again — keyword submit, with original_search
  • Other Ways to Sell Your Items to B&H — label: Online Quote Form, Email Us, Trade In Counter
  • add to basket — with tradein_item_id
  • details · checkout — the two quote-summary links
  • customer information updated — address saved

For comparison, the product listing page declares more than thirty tracked components and the PDP more than forty. Every one of the seven above sits on navigation or a link; not one measures a decision the seller makes about their own item. That is the shape of the problem — the flow is instrumented for movement, not for behavior.

T3 · reporting

Trade-in sits outside the app's page_type vocabulary

The React app carries a closed enum of fifteen page_type values. None of them covers trade-in. The JSP pages therefore mint their own free-text values — "Used - Find Your Item", "Used - Item Condition" — in a different naming style (spaced title case with a hyphen) from every React value (productList, productDetail). Any funnel that spans PDP → trade-in needs a hand-maintained mapping, and the trade-in values will not appear in any report built off the enum.

React enum home · category · productList · productDetail · cart · checkout · searchZeroResults replacement · modifyOrder · giftIdeas · paybooLandingPage · myAccount · static orderHistoryDetail · leasing Trade-in "Used - Find Your Item" · "Used - Item Condition"
T4 · good news

The PDP callout entry point is already tagged, with the SKU

The impact framework lists the entry-point breakdown — specifically mega menu and PDP callout — as needing instrumentation. For the PDP callout that turns out to be wrong: detail_page_clicks / Trade In - Learn More fires on the in-page "Trade in your gear for cash" unit and carries detail_page_sku. So you can already report which products drive sellers into the funnel, not just how many.

Two caveats. The footer link fires footer_clicks / Trade in Your Gear instead, so a complete entry-point count has to union two event families. And the bundle declares a second component, Trade In Phobio - Learn More, for the third-party trade-in path — on products that surface it, trade-in intent splits across two components and will undercount if a report only matches one.

T5 · continuity

Nothing carries source across the PDP → trade-in boundary

The PDP click knows the SKU. The trade-in landing page_view that follows carries no referring component, no source parameter, and no SKU. Joining the two means reconstructing session paths in GA4 rather than reading a parameter — workable for a one-off analysis, fragile as a recurring metric. Passing the originating SKU or a source token into the landing page would make PDP-attributed trade-in volume a direct query.

T6 · identification

The configurator page_view doesn't say what's being quoted

The only event on the highest-intent page in the funnel carries no SKU, no product name, and no offer value. Even without new click tagging, adding the item identifier and quote amount to this page_view would immediately yield quote-value distribution and category mix — the "median quote value" and "quote by category" cuts that currently have to come out of the quote system rather than GA4.

T8 · critical

The conversion event carries an internal ID and nothing else

add to basket is the moment a quote comes into existence — the closest thing this funnel has to a purchase. It sends one meaningful parameter: tradein_item_id: 70676. No quote value, no condition, no product name, no category. It is not a GA4 ecommerce event either, so there is no value, no currency, no items[] — meaning trade-in volume cannot be valued alongside anything else in GA4.

What makes this the easiest fix on the list: all three missing values are already in the URL of the very request that fires it.

/c/tradeIn/addToBasket?itemCondition=450&accessories=&usersPrice=230&id=70676 fires → tradein_page_clicks { component: "add to basket", tradein_item_id: "70676" } missing → usersPrice (230) itemCondition (450) accessories (empty)

Mapping usersPrice to value, itemCondition to label_value, and the product name to label would deliver quote-value distribution, condition mix and category mix in one change — and it needs no new event, only three more parameters on an event that already fires.

T9 · critical

"Used - Confirmation" fires when nothing has been confirmed

The page_type sequence through the funnel runs: Used - Find Your Item → Used - Item Condition → Used - Confirmation → Used - Review Quote. The value named "Confirmation" fires on the add-to-basket transition, before the seller has reviewed anything, entered an address, or submitted. The genuinely final step is the one called "Review Quote."

Anyone building a funnel report in good faith will treat "Confirmation" as the completion step and will overstate submitted trade-ins by everyone who adds an item and walks away — which, given the address form sits after it, is likely a substantial share. If any existing dashboard or KPI already counts this value as a completion, its trade-in numbers are wrong and have been for as long as the naming has been in place.

T10 · critical

The last step before submission is entirely untagged

On the review screen, exactly five elements carry a data-tracking attribute — details, checkout, and the three "Other Ways to Sell" links. Not among them: Enter Information (the primary CTA, which opens the address modal), Log In, Edit, Remove, and Add More Items. Opening the address modal fires no event and no page_view, so the seller stays on Used - Review Quote throughout.

So the drop-off between "quote created" and "address entered" — the single most actionable number in the funnel, and the one that tells you whether the friction is the offer or the form — has no event marking either boundary.

T11 · leverage

Universal Analytics is capturing the context GA4 is missing

The trade-in pages still run Universal Analytics (UA-48894505-1) alongside GA4, and it is set up far more richly. On the add-to-basket step it sets sixty-plus custom dimensions, including several that answer questions GA4 currently cannot:

  • dimension62 — the referring page, https://www.bhphotovideo.com/find/trade-in.jsp (this is exactly the cross-surface source that T5 says GA4 lacks)
  • dimension8 — the previous URL, including the original search query
  • dimension68 — the full breadcrumb path
  • dimension12 — page type, mirroring the GA4 value

Two implications. First, the source-continuity fix is a port rather than a build — the values are already being computed on the page. Second, roughly fifty of those dimensions are being sent as the literal string "null", and the whole UA property is collecting on a platform that no longer processes data, so this is dead weight that also happens to hold the map for the GA4 work.

T7 · hygiene

Three smaller inconsistencies worth folding into the same ticket

  • Null sentinels disagree. Trade-in sends bi: "undefined"; the React app sends bi: "NoValue". Neither is empty, so both pollute reports differently.
  • Client-ID param renamed. Trade-in sends google_client_id; everywhere else it is g_client_id. Two custom dimensions for one value.
  • label_value is unused across all three surfaces. On the listing page exactly one component fills it (Other Buying Options, carrying the SKU). It is the natural home for the condition value, the quote amount, and the gallery image index — all currently missing.
